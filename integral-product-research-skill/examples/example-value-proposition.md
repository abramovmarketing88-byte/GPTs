# Example Value Proposition & Hypothesis Validation Plan

## Scenario

Selected segment: small online schools with paid programs.
Selected job: diagnose learner disengagement and prioritize interventions.

## 1. Selected Segment & Job

| Segment | Job | Painful Moment | Desired Outcome | Current Alternative | Why Unsatisfactory | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small online schools | Когда completion падает, я хочу понять причины disengagement, чтобы улучшить программу и сохранить выручку. | Looking at LMS dashboards without knowing what to change | Clear prioritized interventions | LMS analytics + spreadsheets | Descriptive, slow, low confidence | Low |

## 2. Value Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Segment | Job | Pain | Value Mechanism | Risk | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| For small online schools, the product helps identify the top 3 drop-off causes by combining LMS events, survey prompts, and intervention recommendations. | Small online schools | Diagnose disengagement | Unclear root causes | Automated diagnosis + recommendations | Value risk | Low |
| The product helps program managers reduce analysis time from days to hours by producing a weekly retention brief. | Small online schools | Monitor learner health | Manual analysis | Time-saving report | Usage risk | Low |

## 3. Value Decomposition

| Hypothesis | Functional | Emotional | Economic | Risk Reduction | Cognitive Load |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drop-off diagnosis | Know what to fix | Feel in control | Protect revenue | Avoid wrong interventions | Reduce dashboard interpretation |

## 4. Barriers & Drivers

- Drivers: revenue pressure, refund risk, poor completion metrics, founder concern.
- Barriers: LMS integration, distrust of AI recommendations, limited budget, privacy concerns.
- Trust builders: transparent evidence for each recommendation, human-readable diagnosis, customer examples.

## 5. Riskiest Assumptions

| Assumption | Risk Type | Why Critical | RAT Priority |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Teams urgently need root-cause diagnosis, not just analytics | Problem risk | If false, product is nice-to-have | 90 |
| Teams will pay for recommendations | WTP risk | If false, business model fails | 85 |
| Available data is enough for useful recommendations | Technical/value risk | If false, product cannot deliver | 82 |

## 6. RAT Experiment Plan

| Assumption | Experiment | Metric | Success Criteria | Failure Criteria | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent problem | Problem interview | Recent painful episodes | 8/12 describe recent manual diagnosis and business impact | Fewer than 4 have recent pain | 1 week | Low |
| WTP | Pricing conversation + fake door | Budget signal | 3+ teams request pilot or accept price range | No serious budget signal | 1–2 weeks | Low |
| Data sufficiency | Concierge MVP | Actionable report quality | 3 reports produce accepted recommendations | Reports too generic | 2 weeks | Medium |

## 7. Recommended Value Proposition

Short UVP:

> Find why learners drop off and what to fix next, without spending days in LMS reports.

Positioning:

> For small online schools that need to improve paid course completion, the product is a learner retention assistant that turns fragmented LMS data into prioritized intervention recommendations. Unlike generic dashboards, it explains likely drop-off causes and suggests next actions.

## 8. Messaging

- Headline: `Know why students stop — and what to fix this week.`
- Subheadline: `AI-powered retention briefs for online schools that need actionable completion insights, not another dashboard.`
- CTA: `Get a sample retention brief`.

## 9. Next Experiments

1. Problem interviews.
2. Concierge diagnostic report.
3. Landing page + sample report fake door.

## 10. Decision Rules

- Continue if teams show recent pain, share data, and request pilot.
- Change if pain exists but recommendations need human service layer.
- Defer if data access blocks quick delivery.
- Kill if completion diagnosis is not urgent or budgeted.
